The engineering organisation has fundamentally transformed how our primary transaction service is packaged, distributed, and delivered to our production environments. Previously, our delivery methodology relied entirely on copying loose code artifacts onto individual virtual machines that were assumed to be in a specific, pristine operational state. If a target machine deviated from our strict assumptions regarding software versions or directory structures, the deployment would fail in unpredictable ways, causing unacceptable delays in feature delivery. We have now officially moved to a fully self contained software packaging model, accompanied by an autonomous orchestration platform that actively maintains our desired operational state. This transition represents a significant leap in reliability, scalability, and overall operational maturity for the business, ensuring our customers experience far fewer interruptions during high-traffic periods.

The first major improvement is in how we bundle our software for broad distribution. Our initial attempts at this new packaging model resulted in an artifact size of 847 megabytes, which dangerously included unnecessary development tools, testing frameworks, and raw source code files. By implementing a sophisticated separation between our build environment and our final runtime environment, we achieved a massive reduction in the overall footprint. The final production ready package now measures exactly 78.6 megabytes. This dramatic size reduction significantly accelerates our deployment speed, as substantially less data needs to be transferred across our network architecture during a rolling update. Furthermore, by deliberately stripping out all non essential tools and utilities, we have minimized the potential attack surface of our application, ensuring a much stronger security posture for our sensitive financial transactions.

The second major improvement lies in our new capability for autonomous failure recovery. Under our previous operational model, if the underlying machine or the application process failed, our transaction service would experience a hard outage. An on call engineer would have to receive an automated alert, manually access the affected server, diagnose the underlying issue, and forcefully restart the process. Today, we rely on an intelligent cluster that actively monitors our service health. During our rigorous testing phase, we simulated a catastrophic failure by intentionally destroying the running application instance. The autonomous system immediately detected the absence of the service and provisioned a completely new, healthy replacement. The total time elapsed from the moment of destruction to the replacement instance fully processing transactions was exactly 30 seconds. Because we run multiple instances simultaneously, the service remained available to customers throughout this brief recovery window.

Here is the technical breakdown of how our specific capabilities have evolved between the previous deployment phase and our current resilient architecture:

| Concern | Week 7 | Week 8 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| deployment mechanism | Imperative bash scripts executed over SSH pulling a tarball from Nexus. | Declarative YAML manifests applied via kubectl referencing a tagged Docker image. |
| rollback mechanism | Modifying an nginx upstream configuration to point to a previous port. | Updating the Deployment manifest to reference a previously known good image tag. |
| failure recovery | Manual intervention required if the VM failed. systemd only handled local process crashes. | Automatic recreation and rescheduling of Pods by the ReplicaSet controller across any healthy node. |
| scaling | Manual provisioning of a new VM and updating the central load balancer configuration. | Adjusting the replicas field in the Deployment manifest to provision additional Pods instantly. |

This architectural evolution fundamentally shifts our engineering focus from reactive system maintenance to proactive platform enhancement. By trusting a dedicated control plane to manage and maintain our desired state, our engineers no longer need to perform manual health checks, respond to low level alerts in the middle of the night, or write complex conditional scripts to ensure the software is running correctly. The orchestration system inherently understands that our transaction engine must always have a specific minimum number of instances available to handle customer demand. It will ruthlessly enforce that availability rule, seamlessly routing around degraded hardware and transient network failures. This level of guaranteed resilience is absolutely essential for a modern, high-traffic financial platform and provides the stable foundation necessary for our upcoming global growth initiatives.

However, there remains one critical gap in our current deployment strategy that prevents us from achieving full maturity. Currently, all environmental parameters, such as database connection strings and environment identifiers, are rigidly embedded within the deployment definition itself. This prevents us from promoting the exact same package from a testing environment into production without modifying the core definition. The next phase of our infrastructure evolution will introduce dedicated configuration management and secure credential storage, allowing our operational definitions to remain completely agnostic of their surrounding environment while maintaining strict security boundaries.
